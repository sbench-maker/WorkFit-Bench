# AURORA-Grid Proposal Brief

This is a fictional, locally constructed proposal package for an NSF-style Secure and Resilient Infrastructure competition. It is a three-year request led by Northbridge Institute with a subaward to Cygnus Technical College.

## Research objective

AURORA-Grid will create privacy-preserving anomaly detection for municipal microgrids. The project will build a secure telemetry benchmark, develop a federated detection engine, and validate it at four municipal testbeds without centralizing sensitive operational data.

## Team and work

- Dr. Mira Chen provides scientific leadership, directs the benchmark and field validation, and manages the collaboration.
- Dr. Rafael Ortiz leads federated modeling and evaluation.
- A full-time postdoctoral scholar implements the benchmark, model, and field-validation pipeline.
- A full-time graduate research assistant supports experiments and the annual broader-impacts workshops.
- A project coordinator supports management, workshops, and dissemination for two calendar months per year.
- Cygnus Technical College supplies the cyber-physical test environment and leads testbed validation under Task T3.
- An adversarial-controls consultant provides four days of design review per year.

The two rugged synchronized sensor arrays are required in Year 1 because the current laboratory instruments cannot produce synchronized attack traces across electrical and network channels. The analysis laptop supports field configuration and is dedicated to the project. Secure compute and encrypted backup support all three research tasks. Annual workshops will support eight community-college participants with stipends and travel. Dissemination occurs in Year 3 after integrated field results are available.
