# A queue is not a plan

*Parts Hold starts with the promise you should not make.*

Nine jobs looked ready on Monday morning. Three of them were waiting on parts that had not reached the branch.

The board was technically accurate. Each job had an appointment, a technician, and an open status. It was operationally wrong because the next action depended on inventory living in a different screen.

That gap is where Parts Hold begins.

## Put the constraint beside the commitment

In a five-week pilot, Alder & Finch linked incoming parts to scheduled work across two service hubs. When a required item had no confirmed receipt, the job showed the constraint beside the appointment. Planners could keep the slot, move it, or choose a job with everything on hand.

The product did not make that choice. It made the conflict hard to miss.

Before the pilot, 18% of next-day jobs were reviewed after 3 p.m. because a planner discovered a missing part late. During the pilot, that share was 10%. The median review happened 74 minutes earlier.

Earlier is useful only if someone can act. Alder & Finch paired the flag with a simple branch rule: by 2 p.m., every job with an unconfirmed part needed an owner and one of three decisions—hold the slot, swap the job, or call the customer.

The rule gave the signal teeth. Without it, Parts Hold would have produced a tidier queue and the same late scramble.

## Do not confuse a shipment with a part on the shelf

One edge case shaped the whole pilot. A shipment could show “delivered” at the loading dock while the required item was still unreceived in branch inventory. For a planner, those are different states.

Parts Hold uses the branch receipt, not the carrier event, before it clears the warning. That creates a short period when the carrier says the box arrived and the job still looks constrained. Good. The planner needs the state the branch can act on, not the most optimistic timestamp in the chain.

Alder & Finch found 27 of those mismatches during the pilot. Twenty-three cleared the same day. Four exposed a receiving problem that would otherwise have surfaced when a technician reached for the part.

This is why a workflow product should show its boundary. Parts Hold can tell a planner that the required item is not confirmed on the shelf. It cannot tell them whether the customer will accept Thursday instead of Tuesday. That remains a conversation.

## Read the result at the right size

The pilot covered 612 work orders in two hubs. The late-review rate fell eight percentage points. That is a measured result for this workflow, not a forecast for every service operation.

We are watching two things after launch: whether teams can keep late review below 12%, and whether the branch rule survives peak volume. Those are targets. They are not extra lines in the pilot scorecard.
