## Abstract

Customer-support escalations often arrive with incomplete context, forcing specialists to reconstruct prior troubleshooting. We tested whether generated first drafts, followed by mandatory agent verification, improved handoff efficiency without weakening factual reliability.

Eighteen service teams were randomized by team to a verified-draft workflow or a manual-note workflow for ten weeks. The study included 1,260 eligible escalations across billing, account access, and device-support queues.

Verified drafts reduced median time to resolution by 12.4 percent. Required-field omissions fell from 14.8 percent in manual notes to 6.1 percent in verified drafts. However, final notes in the verified-draft arm contained critical factual errors in 2.7 percent of cases, compared with 0.8 percent for manual notes.

The findings support generated drafting only when the assigned agent reviews and confirms the note before posting. The study did not evaluate automatic posting.

## Introduction

A useful escalation note must identify the customer goal, attempted steps, current state, and promised follow-up. Missing context delays work, while an incorrect account state or commitment can cause direct customer harm.

Earlier observational work mixed unreviewed summaries with reviewed drafts. That makes it difficult to separate the value of drafting from the safety effect of human verification.

Our primary question was whether a generated draft plus mandatory verification changes resolution time. Secondary outcomes were required-field completeness, critical factual-error rate, and agent editing time.

We preregistered the hypothesis that verified drafting would reduce resolution time by at least 8 percent. We did not preregister a non-inferiority margin for factual errors.

## Methods

Teams, rather than individual agents, were randomized to avoid workflow contamination. Nine teams used verified drafts and nine continued manual notes.

The 1,260 escalations comprised 430 billing cases, 390 account-access cases, and 440 device-support cases. High-severity security incidents and non-English conversations were excluded.

The drafting system produced a note from the conversation transcript and ticket fields. Before posting, the assigned agent had to check four required fields and select “confirmed.” Agents could edit or discard the draft.

Independent quality reviewers, blinded to assignment, compared final notes against transcripts and account records. A critical error was a wrong customer identity, product, account state, troubleshooting action, or promised next step.

Resolution time was measured from specialist assignment to escalation closure. The analysis adjusted for queue and baseline team volume.

## Results

Median resolution time was 19.1 hours with verified drafts and 21.8 hours with manual notes, an adjusted reduction of 12.4 percent (95% confidence interval, 7.1% to 17.2%; p < .001).

Required-field omissions occurred in 6.1 percent of verified-draft notes and 14.8 percent of manual notes, an 8.7 percentage-point difference (95% confidence interval, 5.3 to 12.1 points).

Critical factual errors occurred in 2.7 percent of verified-draft final notes and 0.8 percent of manual notes. The study was not powered for this outcome, but the difference remained operationally concerning.

Agents discarded 7.4 percent of generated drafts. Median active editing and review time was 3.6 minutes, compared with 5.2 minutes to compose a manual note.

The resolution-time benefit was concentrated in device support and routine billing. Account-access cases showed a 3.2 percent reduction with a confidence interval spanning zero.

## Discussion

Verified drafting improved speed and completeness, but the higher observed critical-error rate prevents a simple claim of safety equivalence. Mandatory verification reduced, but did not eliminate, incorrect generated content.

Because automatic posting was not tested, these results cannot support removing the confirmation step. A deployment should monitor final-note errors, not only draft acceptance or handling time.

The excluded security and non-English cases limit generalization. Team-level randomization strengthens causal interpretation, though agents knew which workflow they used.

The public funder had no role in study design, analysis, or publication. The drafting system was a research prototype, not a commercial product.

## Limitations

The trial lasted ten weeks, so it does not establish whether benefits persist after novelty and training effects fade. It covered one support network and three queues.

No privacy, retention, or multilingual evaluation was conducted. Customer satisfaction was exploratory and underpowered.

Critical errors were assessed in final posted notes. The study did not separately publish the error rate of raw, unreviewed drafts.

## Conclusion

Generated drafts with mandatory agent verification can shorten escalation resolution and improve required-field completeness. The observed error difference and excluded populations justify a controlled rollout with error auditing rather than default automatic publication.
