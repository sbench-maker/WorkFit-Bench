# Campaign brief: Dispatch Windows operator issue

## Reader and job

Write for regional operations leaders who run several service branches. They know dispatch vocabulary but have not seen Dispatch Windows. The issue should help them decide whether to bring the feature into a weekly operations review.

## Story spine

- Open on one measured pilot result, not on a market trend or product announcement.
- Explain the recurring scheduling problem before explaining the feature.
- Show how Dispatch Windows changes the handoff between coordinators, dispatchers, and technicians.
- Use the Harborline pilot as evidence. Include the pilot scale, the scheduling-time change, the after-hours reassignment change, and the adoption signal from the final fortnight.
- Treat on-time arrival as a useful secondary result. Treat the missed-appointment change as directional, not conclusive.
- State the general-availability date and eligible plans.
- Close with a practical next step: ask the reader to bring one week of after-hours reassignments to a Northstar workflow review.

## Evidence rules

`approved_facts.json` controls all numbers, names, dates, plan availability, and usable quotations. The transcript provides texture and workflow detail, but lines marked off record or unverified are not publishable. `launch_notes.md` is a working draft: anything tagged internal, superseded, or unapproved must stay out of the issue.

Targets describe what Northstar intends to sustain after launch. They are not additional Harborline results. Percentage points and relative percent changes are different; use the unit in the approved facts.

## Medium

This is an email newsletter, not a press release. A useful issue has a subject line or title, short paragraphs, restrained section labels, and no footnotes or external links. The only call to action is the workflow review described above.
